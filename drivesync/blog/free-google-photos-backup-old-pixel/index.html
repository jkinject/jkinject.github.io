How to Get Free Google Photos Backup With an Old Pixel Phone

By the makers of DriveSync · 2026-11-26

Every Google Account comes with 15 GB of free storage, and Gmail, Google Drive and Google Photos all share it. If your phone takes a lot of photos, that runs out fast, and the usual answer is a Google One plan.

There is another answer many people have never heard of. Google still lets older Pixel phones back up photos and videos without counting them against that 15 GB. Some people keep an old Pixel at home just for this: they send their photos to it, and it uploads them. Here is how that works, what you need, and what can go wrong.

Why an old Pixel backs up for free

When Google ended free unlimited backup for everyone on 1 June 2021, it kept an exception for older Pixel phones. That exception is still written into Google's own help pages today. About your Google Photos activity & storage says that if you have a Pixel 5 or earlier, you get unlimited storage for photos and videos backed up from your device at no charge.

There is no end date on that page. It is simply the current policy, and Google can change it. So treat it as something that works today, not something Google has promised to keep.

Which Pixel you need

The quality of the free backup depends on the model. This is how Google's backup quality page (Android tab) puts it, checked on 26 November 2026:

PhoneWhat is free
Pixel (1st generation, 2016)Unlimited, Original quality
Pixel 2, Pixel 3Unlimited, Storage saver (for anything backed up after the cut-off dates in 2021 and 2022)
Pixel 3a, 4, 4a, 5Unlimited, Storage saver
Pixel 5a, Pixel 6 and laterNothing extra. Backups count toward your storage

Watch the names. A Pixel 5 qualifies, a Pixel 5a does not. If your model has a variant name (XL, 5G), check it against Google's page before you rely on it.

Storage saver is a compressed copy. Google says photos larger than 16 MP are resized to 16 MP and videos above 1080p are resized to 1080p. For everyday phone photos the difference is hard to spot. For 4K video it is a real downgrade. Only the first-generation Pixel keeps the original file for free.

The exact cut-off dates are in the full table in our OneDrive-to-Pixel guide.

What you need

  • A qualifying Pixel. One you already own, or a used one. It does not need a SIM card, only Wi-Fi.
  • The same Google Account on the Pixel as the one your photos should end up in.
  • Backup turned on in Google Photos on the Pixel: tap your profile picture, then Photos settings, Backup.
  • The right quality. On a Pixel 2 to 5, set Photos & videos quality (on the same Backup screen) to Storage saver. Original quality backups count toward your storage.
  • A charger and Wi-Fi. The Pixel does the uploading, so it needs to stay on.

Getting photos from your main phone onto the Pixel

This is the part people find fiddly. Google's wording is "backed up from your device", so the photos have to reach the Pixel first and be uploaded by it. Copying them into Google Photos from a computer or a newer phone is a normal upload and uses your storage.

People usually do it in one of three ways.

1. A sync app between the two phones

Apps such as Syncthing or FolderSync can copy new photos from your main phone to the Pixel over Wi-Fi, mostly on their own once set up. We have not tested them for this guide, so follow their documentation. Two things to check:

  • Put the photos where Google Photos will pick them up. It backs up the camera folder, DCIM/Camera, automatically. Any other folder needs to be switched on under Back up device folders in the Pixel's Google Photos settings, as Google's backup page explains.
  • If synced photos do not show up in Google Photos at all, Android may not have indexed them as media yet. A thread on the Syncthing forum, Images are not indexed at Android OS level, deals with exactly this.

2. A computer and a USB cable

Copy the photos from your main phone to a computer, then into DCIM/Camera on the Pixel. It is manual, but it needs no extra apps and you see exactly what you copied.

3. A cloud service in between

If your main phone already uploads to another cloud, such as OneDrive or Dropbox, you can pull the photos from that cloud onto the Pixel and let Google Photos take it from there. This is also how people empty a full OneDrive into Google Photos.

Whichever route you choose, turn Backup off in Google Photos on your main phone if it uses the same Google Account. Otherwise the main phone uploads its own copy too, and that copy counts toward your storage.

The catches

  • Photos not taken on the Pixel. Google's help page talks about photos "backed up from your device" and, in its Pixel section, about photos "you take on your Pixel". It does not say outright whether photos that came from another phone are covered. So test it yourself before moving everything: note the storage figure in your Google Account, back up a few hundred photos through the Pixel, and check a day later that the figure has not gone up.
  • Compression. On a Pixel 2 to 5 the free copy is Storage saver. If the originals matter, keep them on a computer or an external drive as well.
  • Google can change the terms. It has before: the Pixel 2 lost free original quality in January 2021, everyone else lost free unlimited backup in June 2021, and the Pixel 3 lost free original quality in January 2022. Check the help page for your model now and then.
  • Inactive accounts. The same Google page says photos might be deleted if you do not use Google Photos for two years or more. Open Google Photos on the Pixel from time to time.
  • An old phone, always plugged in. None of these models still gets Android or security updates from Google, according to Google's update page. Use it only as an upload box, not as your everyday phone, and keep an eye on the battery, since old batteries can swell.
  • Phone storage. When a batch has finished uploading, use Free up space in Google Photos to remove local copies that are already backed up, then send the next batch.
