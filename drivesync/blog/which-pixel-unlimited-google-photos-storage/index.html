Which Pixel Phones Still Get Unlimited Google Photos Storage? A Model-by-Model Check

By the makers of DriveSync · 2026-11-27

Short answer: the first Pixel from 2016 and every Pixel up to the Pixel 5 still back up photos and videos to Google Photos without using your storage. The Pixel 5a and everything after it do not. Only the first-generation Pixel keeps original quality for free; the rest get Storage saver, a compressed copy.

The questions people get stuck on are the ones around that: is a Pixel 4a (5G) covered, does an XL count, how do you tell a Pixel 5 from a 5a, and are your uploads really free. Here are the answers from Google's own help pages, re-checked on 27 November 2026.

What Google's help pages say

Two pages set the rules. About your Google Photos activity & storage says that with a Pixel 5 or earlier you get unlimited storage for photos and videos backed up from your device at no charge. Choose the backup quality of your photos & videos (open the Android tab) gives the detail per group of models:

PhoneWhat Google says is free
Pixel (1st generation, 2016)Unlimited in Original quality. You can't choose Storage saver on this phone
Pixel 2Backed up in Original quality on or before 16 January 2021: stays Original, free. After that date: saved in Storage saver, free
Pixel 3Backed up in Original quality on or before 31 January 2022: stays Original, free. After that date: saved in Storage saver, free
Pixel 3a to Pixel 5Storage saver is unlimited and free. Original quality uses your storage

The Pixel 5a, the Pixel 6 and later models are not on either page, so backups from them count toward your Google storage like any other phone.

Google can change these terms. Neither page shows an end date today, but check the Android tab of the quality page for your own model before you rely on it.

The variants Google doesn't name

Google writes "Pixel 3a-5" as a range. It does not list each model, and it never mentions the XL versions on its Photos pages. Here is how the in-between phones line up. The model names are the ones Google uses on its Pixel update page.

Google's update page groups each XL with its smaller sibling ("Pixel 3 & Pixel 3 XL"), and owners generally treat them as the same case. Still, the Photos page doesn't say so in words. If you own one of the unnamed models, run the small test further down before you send it your whole library.

Pixel 5, Pixel 5a, Pixel 4a (5G): the naming trap

The confusing part is "5G". The Pixel 4a (5G) is a 2020 phone inside Google's range. The Pixel 5a is sold as "Pixel 5a with 5G" and is a 2021 phone outside it. A listing that says "Pixel 5a 5G" is the one that does not come with free backup, even though it sounds newer and better.

For a vague second-hand listing ("Pixel 5 series", "Pixel 4a/5"), ask for a screenshot of the model name before you buy.

How to tell which Pixel you have

  1. Open Settings on the phone and tap About phone.
  2. Look at Model. Ignore Device name: that is a label the owner can change, so a phone named "Pixel 5" can be anything.
  3. If the phone won't turn on, the model is printed on the box.

There is no switch to turn on

People look for an "unlimited" toggle. There isn't one. On a qualifying Pixel the free backup applies to what that phone backs up, as long as two things are set in Google Photos on the Pixel:

  • Backup is on. Tap your profile picture, then Google Photos settings, Backup.
  • The quality matches your model. On the same screen, under Photos & videos quality, a Pixel 3a to 5 should be on Storage saver. If it is on Original quality, those backups use your storage. A first-generation Pixel only offers Original quality, which is the free option there.

Google notes that you can't change the quality once you're out of storage, so set this before your account fills up.

How to check that your backups really are free

Google's wording is photos "backed up from your device". It does not say whether photos taken on another phone and copied onto the Pixel count the same way, so check your own account:

  1. In Google Photos, tap your profile picture and open Manage storage. This opens the Google One storage manager.
  2. Tap Usage details and write down how much Google Photos is using.
  3. Back up a small batch through the Pixel, a few hundred photos.
  4. Check the figure again a day later. If it hasn't gone up, the batch was free.

If you plan to feed the Pixel with photos from your main phone or a cloud, run this test with exactly those photos.

Buying a used Pixel for this

  • Check the model in About phone, as above.
  • Make sure the seller removed their Google Account. Google's device protection page says that after a factory reset you won't be able to use the phone at all if you can't sign in with an account that was on it before.
  • Expect no updates. Every model on the list, from the Pixel 1 to the Pixel 5a, no longer gets Android or security updates, according to Google's update page. Use it as an upload box on your home Wi-Fi, not as your everyday phone.
  • Look at the battery. It will sit on a charger a lot, and old batteries can swell. No SIM card is needed.

Where to go from here

Remember that on every model except the first Pixel the free copy is Storage saver: Google resizes photos over 16 MP to 16 MP and videos above 1080p to 1080p. If the originals matter, keep them on a computer or a drive as well.
